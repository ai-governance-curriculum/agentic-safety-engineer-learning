# exercise-07 — Certifications and hiring-signal planner

**Estimated effort:** 1 hours
**Prerequisite chapters:** 06 (primary); 02, 03, 04 (source of the specific artefacts the hiring signal is composed of — tier memos, system cards, AISI submissions).

## Objective

Author your **own 12–24 month certifications-and-hiring-signal plan** against the four-certification portfolio and the five-signal portfolio chapter 06 names. The plan is a personal career artefact — a certifications choice with rationale, a hiring-signal choice with concrete artefacts and surfacing mechanisms, a boundary-awareness map against the four neighbouring roles chapter 06 pins, and a 90-day starter checklist that turns the plan into next-quarter actions. The exercise is planning-shape, not authoring-at-scale; it closes mod-112 by forcing you to name your own trajectory against the module's boundary chapter.

## Problem statement

Chapter 06's load-bearing claim is that the safety-engineer's craft (mod-102 through mod-111), the certifications portfolio (AIGP + one of 42001 LI/LA / ForHumanity IAA / BABL AI), and the frontier-safety hiring signal (contributions to a published RSP / Preparedness / FSF determination, published system-card evidence, AISI methodology contributions, peer-reviewed publications, FMF working-group contributions) all compose — none replaces the others. The chapter also pins the four neighbouring roles (`ai-evaluation-engineer` at level 35, `senior-ai-governance-architect` at level 50, `head-of-ai-governance` at level 60, `chief-ai-officer` at level 70) so the safety-engineer can locate their next promotion boundary and avoid over-reach / under-reach.

Most level-40 safety-engineers get the plan wrong on the first attempt in one of two symmetric ways: they over-index on certifications (a portfolio of three certifications and no cited artefacts) or over-index on artefacts (a stack of internal system-card contributions with no disclosure permission and no vocabulary layer). Chapter 06's *"certifications are vocabulary; craft is engineering; the hiring signal is what surfaces the safety-engineer to the roles that let them operate at frontier scope"* is the frame the plan is authored against.

The exercise's discipline is to force **personal inputs**: your jurisdiction, your current employer type (frontier lab / enterprise safety team / consulting / academic / independent), your current portfolio state, and the 12–24 month trajectory you are planning against. Without these inputs the plan is generic; with them it is defensible.

## Requirements

Produce four artefacts. Each is small, personal, and planning-shape.

### Artefact A — `hiring-plan-certifications.md`

Your certifications-portfolio decision per chapter 06's *certifications portfolio* section.

- **Personal-input header.** Jurisdiction (EU / UK / US / other, name the sub-jurisdiction that matters — Ireland, California, Singapore), current employer type, current certification state.
- **Primary.** IAPP AIGP by default per chapter 06's *primary* row. If you defer or substitute, name the reason against chapter 06's *"most-cited; provides shared vocabulary with executives and boards"* signal. Target completion window (quarter + year). Cost budget <!-- needs-research: pin current AIGP registration and exam fees against your jurisdiction. -->.
- **Secondary.** One of ISO/IEC 42001 lead-implementer, 42001 lead-auditor, ForHumanity IAA, or BABL AI. Justify against chapter 06's *"where it fits"* row for the chosen certification and against your jurisdiction / employer-type header. Name the specific training pathway you will use <!-- needs-research: pin the recognised LI / LA training providers (PECB, BSI, TÜV, others) and the IAA / BABL AI current programme structures. -->. Target completion window. Cost budget.
- **Tertiary (optional).** A second certification in the secondary tier if your cross-organisation exposure warrants it (chapter 06's *tertiary* row). If you omit, state the reason — chapter 06 explicitly frames tertiary as optional.
- **What each certification does not replace.** For each of your chosen certifications, quote chapter 06's *"what it does not replace"* row and state how the mod-102 through mod-111 craft you already carry supplies the gap.

### Artefact B — `hiring-plan-signals.md`

Your hiring-signal-portfolio decision per chapter 06's *frontier-safety hiring signal* section.

- **Personal-input header.** Current portfolio state — enumerate any existing contributions (named tier memos, named system cards, AISI publications you contributed to, publications, FMF working-group participation).
- **Signal choice.** At least one of signal 1 (RSP / Preparedness / FSF contribution history) or signal 2 (published system-card evidence). At least one of signal 3 (AISI methodology contributions) or signal 4 (peer-reviewed publication in safety-adjacent venues). Optionally signal 5 (FMF working-group contribution) as differentiator. State the four-of-five slots your plan covers and the reason for the substitution / omission pattern.
- **Concrete artefact per signal.** For each chosen signal, name a **specific artefact** you intend to pursue in the 12–24 month window — not *"a system-card contribution"* but *"the safety-eval-results section of the next Claude family system card"* or the equivalent at your employer. If you are outside a frontier lab, name the substitute (an AISI-adjacent peer-reviewed publication, an FMF working-group input via your organisation's membership, a Preparedness-shape scorecard contribution routed via an academic collaborator).
- **Surfacing mechanism per artefact.** How the artefact surfaces as hiring signal — chapter 06's *"how it surfaces"* rows are the schema. Attribution rules (named contributor list versus internal-attribution-only), publication venue (SATML / IEEE S&P / USENIX Security / NeurIPS-ICML safety tracks / ICLR SafeGenAI / arXiv for signal 4; specific AISI publication for signal 3). Chapter 06's rule that *"published contributions require the organisation's disclosure permission"* is load-bearing — name the disclosure-permission conversation that will happen before the contribution is made and the shape that makes permission likely.
- **12–24 month timeline.** Quarter-level milestones per artefact. Specific gate per artefact (submission deadline, publication cycle, working-group cadence).

### Artefact C — `hiring-plan-boundaries.md`

Your boundary-awareness map against the four neighbouring roles chapter 06 pins.

- One block per neighbouring role: `ai-evaluation-engineer` (peer, level 35, Governance), `senior-ai-governance-architect` (level 50), `head-of-ai-governance` (level 60), `chief-ai-officer` (level 70).
- Per block:
  - **Owns.** One sentence from chapter 06's *"owns"* row for that role.
  - **Boundary.** One sentence from chapter 06's *"boundary"* row — what mod-112 does not do.
  - **Artefacts already produced that surface toward this role.** From your Artefact B portfolio state, name which artefacts move you toward that role's craft.
  - **Gap artefacts.** What is missing before that role is plausible — craft you do not yet carry, artefacts you have not yet produced, signature slots you have not yet held.
  - **Promotion-boundary criterion.** The one criterion that separates *"level-40 safety-engineer with adjacent exposure"* from *"level-N candidate"* for that neighbour. For most learners at level 40, only one of the four is the *next* promotion boundary; name which one and defend the choice against your Artefact A / B trajectory.

### Artefact D — `hiring-plan-90-day.md`

A 90-day starter checklist — first-quarter actions that advance the plan.

- **Certification registration deadlines.** For the primary and (if in-window) secondary, the registration-deadline date and the study-block reservation.
- **Publication draft milestones.** For any signal-4 artefact, the abstract-submission and full-draft milestones against the target venue's cycle.
- **Disclosure-permission conversations.** For any signal-1 / signal-2 / signal-5 artefact whose surfacing depends on the organisation's disclosure permission (chapter 06's *published contributions require the organisation's disclosure permission* rule), the conversation calendared with the named counterparty — legal, comms, the RSO / Preparedness Committee / FSF-equivalent lead, the FMF working-group liaison.
- **AISI methodology inquiries.** For any signal-3 artefact, the inquiry channel — direct bilateral if your organisation has one, or the peer-reviewed-publication route chapter 06 names as the alternative for engineers whose employer lacks a bilateral relationship.
- **FMF working-group inquiries.** If signal 5 is in your plan, the working-group scoping conversation with your organisation's FMF representative <!-- needs-research: verify FMF membership rules for smaller organisations and the observer-participation pathway. -->.
- **Boundary review checkpoint.** A 90-day retrospective slot to re-read Artefact C and confirm the next-promotion-boundary criterion has moved.

## Starter guidance

- **Read chapter 06 end-to-end before drafting.** The *certifications portfolio*, *frontier-safety hiring signal*, *portfolio composition*, and *common misreadings to avoid* sections are the whole scaffold for this exercise. A plan authored without them collapses into a generic training list.
- **The personal-input headers are load-bearing.** A plan without a named jurisdiction, employer type, and current portfolio state is generic. Chapter 06's four certifications and five signals are chosen against those inputs; the exercise's discipline is to force them.
- **Do not commit this to the course repo.** This is genuinely personal career-planning material. It goes in your personal notes or a private repo; the deliverable-location section below is why.
- **Name specific artefacts, not artefact classes.** *"A system-card contribution"* is a class; *"the deployment-mitigations section of the next Gemini family card"* is an artefact. The hiring-manager conversation chapter 06 describes reads at artefact altitude.
- **Disclosure-permission negotiation is a first-class step, not a footnote.** Chapter 06's *common misreadings* list explicitly frames disclosure permission as a first-class artefact-authoring step. The 90-day checklist calendars the conversation; the signals plan structures the artefact so permission is likely.
- **The neighbouring-role map is not a promotion plan; it is a boundary plan.** Chapter 06 pins four neighbours; the exercise names which one is your next boundary. The other three are boundaries you must not cross into (over-reach) and must not defer to (under-reach) at your current level.
- **AIGP defaults are defaults, not commandments.** Chapter 06 names AIGP as the primary default; if your jurisdiction or employer type makes a different certification a better primary (a heavily ISO-shop enterprise makes 42001 LI a defensible primary), justify the substitution against chapter 06's *where it fits* row rather than silently swapping.
- **Do not invent facts about the certifications.** Curriculum content, exam blueprints, and pricing shift; mark unverified specifics `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` rather than asserting them. The resources file already carries the primary URLs.
- **Signal 5 is a differentiator, not a floor.** Chapter 06's portfolio composition frames FMF working-group contribution as *optional*; if you cannot plausibly land it in 12–24 months, omit it and strengthen signals 1–4.
- **Chapters 02, 03, and 04 are the source of the artefacts.** Signal 1 artefacts are tier-decision-memo sections (chapter 02). Signal 2 artefacts are system-card sections (chapter 03). Signal 3 artefacts are AISI submissions and methodology contributions (chapter 04). The plan cites the chapters as the source of the craft the signal reports on.

## Stretch goals

- **Map the plan to a specific target-employer JD.** Pick a frontier-lab safety-engineering JD (or a plausible surrogate) and mark, per line item of the JD, which of your Artefact A / B / C entries maps to it. The exercise is diagnostic; a JD line item with no mapping is a gap.
- **Draft the disclosure-permission ask for one signal-2 artefact.** A short (~200 word) memo to the counterparty (legal, comms, the RSO equivalent) that names the artefact, the surfacing, the risk, and the mitigation. Chapter 06's over-reach failure mode is why.
- **Add a 24-month review checkpoint.** Beyond the 90-day retrospective, a 24-month checkpoint where the entire plan is re-authored against the certifications-programme revisions, the frontier-lab hiring-signal drift, and your own portfolio state at that time.
